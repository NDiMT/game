# Level 6: ten ideas from a large research (24/11)

The owner turned down the toppling columns and some thirty ideas after them ("no, come on, you can do better, do a big
research"). Three research passes were run: inventive puzzle-game mechanics (2008-2026), puzzle-design theory with
classic recreational puzzles, and Greek myths with ancient Greek machines. Their common lesson: a puzzle feels clever
when its answer follows from rules the player already knows but the player's first assumption hides it; the obvious
move fails where it can be seen to fail (Mark Brown, Alan Hazelden, Jonathan Blow, Bob Bates, Scott Kim).

The ten ideas offered, each painted by the image model in the game's style (`gen.py`, the level 2 and level 5
concepts as references; `prompt_base.txt` and `prompt_<name>.txt`), shown to the owner as a page:

Kept from the research for later: the Clashing Rocks (send a decoy, cross as they reopen; still forever after), the

in a known order, a stone skipped over still water whose wake moves rafts, Proteus held fast, linked cisterns
(Aeneas's hydraulic telegraph), Halcyon's wind-mouths, Lights Out slabs, chess tempo (one odd loop shifts the beat).
