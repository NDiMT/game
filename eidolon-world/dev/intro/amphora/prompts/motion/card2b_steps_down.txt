The two attached pictures are the FIRST FRAME and the LAST FRAME of the video.
Bring this ancient Greek black-figure vase painting to life as flat 2D cut-out animation, as if the painted figures on
the vase began to move. LOCKED-OFF STATIC CAMERA: no zoom, no pan, no tilt, no camera movement at all. The orange clay
ground, the black bands at the left and right, and everything that is not named below stay perfectly still.
The drawing stays exactly as painted: the same flat colours, the same outlines and incised lines, no shading, no 3D,
no light effects, no new objects, no new figures, no text. Single continuous shot, no cuts, no fades, no dissolves.
Silent: no music, no voices.

WHAT HAPPENS:
- The young woman in the red dress stands on the low black block (first picture). For the first half second she
  stands still. Then she steps down off the block to the right: one foot down onto the ground, then the other, one
  small step more, and she stands still exactly where she is in the last picture. That is all she does.
- She moves like a real person stepping down one low step: natural, smooth, even and unhurried, with the weight
  passing from foot to foot. No slow motion, no sliding, no gliding, no jerks, no pauses in the middle.
- She is standing still in her final place for the whole last second and a half.
- She stays upright, IN STRICT PROFILE FACING RIGHT the whole time; she never turns toward the viewer and makes no
  gestures. Her feet stay bare.
- HER FACE STAYS A BLANK, SMOOTH, EMPTY WHITE SHAPE THE WHOLE TIME: no eyes, no eyebrows, no nose, no mouth.
- The black block does not move, slide or change. The kneeling man, the goddess with her mirror, the dove and the
  temple do not move at all.
